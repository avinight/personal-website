# Plausibility

Plausibility means either highly probable or very reasonable to say. Highly probable constructs denote a statistical likelihood for that construct to be true. Reasonable to say constructs denote when the mapping to and from that construct is made clear, taken seriously, and is sensibly justified. We must hence be careful to avoid [[Equivocation | equivocating]] on this word. 
    
Intermodal information involves creating constructs that have multiple lines of inference towards those constructs that ultimately explain how we do science. More often than not, an imbalance in these lines of inference impedes scientific progress. Farfetchedness uses little lines of inference to produce high elegance. Triviality has high convergence, yet produces little elegance. Only by having a balance of significant proportion to the lines of inference produce profound knowledge.

Deepities are constructs that look profound but are the result of equivocation between a trivial construct and a farfetched one. Avoiding deepities are difficult to identify, but necessary to validate some construct.

Theories of categorization are constructs that we need to determine are profound. Often, some shared assumption presupposes the theories we make. Presuppositions are closely related to plausibility in that they offer knowledge which we can use to generate new theories.
