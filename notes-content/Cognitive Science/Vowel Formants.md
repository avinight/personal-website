# Vowel Formants

![[Screen Shot 2021-12-10 at 6.25.25 PM.png]]
	Vowels F1 - F4
	ɪ: 500, 2300, 3000, 4400
	ɛ: 700, 2100, 3100, 4500
	æ: 800, 1800, 3000, 4500
	ʊ: 500, 1000-1500, 3000, 4300
	
F1 distribution inversely correlates with the articulatory height of the vowel
- High tongue, low F1 [i, u]
- Low tongue, high F1 [ae, a]

F2 height correlates roughly with backness of the vowel
- More front, high F2
- More back, low F2
![[The pattern of the first two formants for the simple vowels of English.png]]

Lip rounding lowers F2, why?
- If you are pronouncing a back vowel, it will have a fairly low F2. If you round your lips at the same time, you will lower F2 even more. This is why it is nearly universal for languages to have rounded back vowels and unrounded front vowels. Lip rounding exaggerates the acoustic effect of backness, and helps make the back vowels more easily distinguishable from front vowels.
