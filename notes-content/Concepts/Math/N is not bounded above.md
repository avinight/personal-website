Proof. Suppose for a contradiction $\mathbb{N}$ was bounded above. Since $\mathbb{N} \neq \emptyset$, there would be a least upper bound $\alpha$ for $\mathbb{N}$. Then
$$\alpha \geq n$$
for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$.
Consequently, 
$$\alpha \geq n + 1$$
for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, since $n + 1$ is in $\mathbb{N}$, if $n$ is in $\mathbb{N}$. 
But this means that
$$\alpha - 1 \geq n$$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$,
and this menas that $\alpha - 1$ is also an upper bound for $\mathbb{N}$, contradicting the fact that $\alpha$ is the least upper bound.
