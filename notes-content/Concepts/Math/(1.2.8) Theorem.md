(1.2.8) Theorem. Let V be a vector space, and let W be a nonempty subset of V. Then W is a subspace of V if and only if for all $x, y \in W$, and all $c \in \R$, we have $cx + y \in W$

Proof. "$\rightarrow$"
- If W is a subspace of V, then for all $x \in W$ and all $c \in \R$, we have $cx \in W$, and hence for all $y \in W, cx + y \in W$ as well, because by definition of subspace W of V must be closed under vector sums and scalar multiples.

Proof. "$\leftarrow$"
- Let W be any subset of V satisfying the condition of the theorem.

	Vector Sums
	- Since $cx + y \in W$ for all choices of $x, y \in W$ and $c \in \R$, we may specialize to the case $c = 1$
	- Then we see that $1x + y = x + y \in W$, so that W is closed under sums.

	Non-emptiness
	- Next let $x = y$ be any vector in W and $c = -1$.
	- Then, $-1x + x = (-1 + 1)x = 0x = 0 \in W$

	Scalar Multiples
	- Now let x be any vector in W and y = 0
	- Then, $cx + 0 = cx \in W$, so W is closed under scalar multiplication

W is a vector space
	- 1, 2, 5-8 axioms are satisfied automatically for vectors in W, since they hold for all vectors in V. 
	- Axiom 3 is satisifed since $0 \in W$
	- Axiom 4 is satisifed since by proposition (1.1.6d)
		$$(-1)x = -x \in W$$
