Let $B = \{\vec{b}_1, \cdots, \vec{b}_n\}$ be a basis for a subspace V and let $\vec{v} \in V$.

The representation of $\vec{v}$ in the B basis, $[\vec{v}]_\mathcal{B}$ is the column matrix
$$
[\vec{v}]_B = \begin{bmatrix} \alpha_1 \\ \vdots \\ \alpha_n \end{bmatrix} 
$$

where $\alpha_1, \cdots, \alpha_n$ uniquely satisfy $\vec{v} = \alpha_1 \vec{b}_1 + \cdots + \alpha_n \vec{b}_n$

Conversely,
$$\begin{bmatrix} \alpha_1 \\ \vdots \\ \alpha_n \end{bmatrix}_\mathcal{B} = \alpha_1 \vec{b}_1 + \cdots + \alpha_n \vec{b}_n$$

is notation for the linear combination of $\vec{b}_1, \cdots, \vec{b}_n$ with coeffiecients $\alpha_1, \cdots, \alpha_n$.

Beta is used to show that $\vec{v}$ is written as a linear combination of vectors in the B basis.
