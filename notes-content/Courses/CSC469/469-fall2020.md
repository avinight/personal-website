# Question 3
---
Metrics:
+ Throughput is likely an important metric for an internet service (improving throughput is good if you are going to adopt the new system).
- Throughput isn’t the only metric that matters. You probably also care about request latency, and perhaps other things like CPU utilization (how much load could one server handle?), number of dropped or rejected requests, and fairness (i.e., what if you have improved throughput by handling lots of small, simple requests but more complex requests are eﬀectively starved).

Workload:
+ Choosing a workload that is representative of the actual client load on your service is good.
- Average load is not very useful. An internet service likely has variation over the course of a day or a week, and may see sudden demand spikes. Seeing how the systems compare under peak load (or overload) is more useful.

Experiments:
+ They have a real measurement of the two systems.
- There is nothing to suggest they have more than a single measurement of the target workload on each of the two systems. They need to conduct enough trials to make sure the results are statistically significant.

Presentation
+ Presenting the results visually (as a graph) is good. The y-axis starts at 0, so the diﬀerences are not visually exaggerated (also good).

- No y-axis label (graph is titled throughput, but in what units?). No error bars on graph (related to previous point - possibly there is just 1 experiment so no error bars are possible). It is hard to tell exactly what throughput System A achieved - including actual numbers with each bar would be better.
