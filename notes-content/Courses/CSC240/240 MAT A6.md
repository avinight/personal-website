5a)
Our basis vectors can be represents by the following linear combination
$$e_i([k]) = e_i([1])e_1 + e_i([2])e_2 + \cdots +e_i([n])e_n$$
Hence a matrix for $e_i([k])$ relative to the basis $B$ is defined by 
$$_B e_i([k]) = \begin{bmatrix} e_i([1]) \\ e_i([2]) \\ \vdots \\e_i([n])\end{bmatrix}$$

To find the entries of a matrix for $\delta$, we apply $\delta$  to each $_B e_i([k])$

The matrix representation of $\delta$ in the given basis is denoted by
$$\begin{align}_B[\delta]_B &= [\delta(_B e_1([k])), \delta(_B e_2([k])), \cdots, \delta(_B e_n([k]))] \\
&= [(\delta e_1)([k]), (\delta e_2)([k]), \cdots, (\delta e_n)([k])]\end{align}$$

the value of $\delta$ on the $j$th basis element $e_j$ of $B$ is
$$\delta(e_j) = \sum_{k = 1}^n \delta(e_j([k]))= \sum_{k = 1}^n (\delta e_j)([k])$$
If $k = j$, then its entries are

$\delta e_j([j]) = e_j([j]) - e_j([j - 1]) = 1 - 0 = 1$ 

If $k = j + 1$, then its entries are

$\delta e_j([j + 1]) = e_j([j + 1]) - e_j([j]) = 0 - 1 = - 1$ 

If $j = n, k = 1$

$\delta e_j([1]) = e_n([1]) - e_n([0]) = -1$ Since $[n] = [0]$ in $\Z_n$

Otherwise, there is no term where $e_i([k]) = 1$. So,

$\delta e_j([k]) = e_j([k]) - e_j([k-1]) = 0$

We hence retrieve the matrix
$$\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & -1\\
-1 & 1 & \ddots & &\ddots&0 \\
0 & -1 & 1 &   & &0\\
\vdots & & \ddots & \ddots&&\vdots\\
0 & \ddots & & \ddots & \ddots &0\\
0 & 0&\cdots&0&-1&1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

$$\delta^{-1}\delta = I_V \mbox{ and } \delta\delta^{-1} = I_V$$

$a_1\delta(e_1) + \cdots + a_n\delta(e_n) = 0$
$-a_1\delta(e_1) - \cdots - a_{n-1}\delta(e_{n-1}) = a_n\delta(e_n)$

We may find a formula for $\delta^k$ by noticing that some examples follow a pattern.
$$
\begin{align}(\delta^2f)([g]) &= (\delta \delta f)([g]) \\
&= \delta (f([g]) - f([g-1])) \\
&= \delta(f([g]) ) - \delta(f([g-1])) \\
&= f([g]) - 2f([g - 1]) +f([g-2])
\end{align}
$$
$$
\begin{align}(\delta^3f)([g]) &= (\delta \delta \delta f)([g]) \\
&= \delta\delta (f([g]) - f([g-1])) \\
&= \delta\delta(f([g]) ) - \delta\delta(f([g-1])) \\
&= \delta(f([g])) - 2\delta(f([g - 1])) + \delta(f([g-2]))\\
&= f([g]) - f([g-1]) - 2f([g-1]) + 2f([g-2]) + f([g-2]) - f([g-3]) \\

\end{align}

A formula for $\delta^k$
$$\delta^kf([g]) = \sum_{j = 0}^k (-1)^j \binom{k}{j} f([g-j])$$
Proof by induction.
Base Case. $k = 2$
$$\begin{align}
\delta^2f([g]) &= \sum_{j = 0}^1(-1)^j \binom{2}{j}f([g - j])\\
&= f([g]) - 2f([g - 1]) +f([g-2])
\end{align}$$

Inductive Step. $\delta^n \implies \delta^{n + 1}$
Assume $$\delta^nf([g]) = \sum_{j = 0}^n (-1)^j \binom{n}{j} f([g-j])$$
Prove that  
$$\delta^{n+1}f([g]) = \sum_{j = 0}^{n+1} (-1)^j \binom{n+1}{j} f([g-j])$$
$$\delta^{n+1}f([g]) = \delta(\delta^{n}f([g]))$$
By induction hypothesis
$$\delta(\delta^n f([g])) = \delta(\sum_{j = 0}^n (-1)^j \binom{n}{j} f([g-j]))$$
Then,
$$\begin{align}\delta(\sum_{j = 0}^n (-1)^j \binom{n}{j} f([g-j])) &= \sum_{j = 0}^n (-1)^j \binom{n}{j} \delta(f([g-j])) \\
&= \sum_{j = 0}^n (-1)^j \binom{n}{j} (f([g-j]) - f([g - j - 1])) \\
&= \sum_{j = 0}^n (-1)^j \binom{n}{j} (f([g-j]) - f([g - j - 1])) \\
&= \sum_{j = 0}^n ((-1)^j  \binom{n}{j}f([g-j]) +(-1)^{j + 1}\binom{n}{j}f([g - (j + 1)])) \\
\end{align}$$
This sum can be rewritten as
$$\begin{align}
&= f([g]) +\sum_{j = 0}^n ((-1)^{j-1}  \binom{n}{j-1}f([g-j])+(-1)^{j}  \binom{n}{j}f([g-j])) + (-1)^{n + 1}\binom{n}{n}f([g - (n + 1)]) \\
&= f([g]) + \sum_{j = 1}^n (-1)^{j-1} (\binom{n}{j-1} + \binom{n}{j})f([g-j]) + (-1)^{n + 1}\binom{n + 1}{n + 1}f([g - (n + 1)]) \\
&= f([g]) + \sum_{j = 1}^n (-1)^{j-1} \binom{n + 1}{j}f([g-j]) + (-1)^{n + 1}\binom{n + 1}{n + 1}f([g - (n + 1)])\\
&= \sum_{j = 0}^{n+1} (-1)^j \binom{n + 1}{j}f([g-j])
\end{align}
$$
Hence, $$\delta^kf([g]) = \sum_{j = 0}^k (-1)^j \binom{k}{j} f([g-j])$$ is true for all $k \geq 2$
